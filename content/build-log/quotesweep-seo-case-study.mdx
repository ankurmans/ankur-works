A closed-beta product does not have to wait until launch day to be discoverable.

QuoteSweep's Google clicks grew **929% from April to August 2026** while the product was still in closed beta. Search Console recorded **96 clicks in April and 988 in August**, or 10.3× the starting monthly count. Search impressions grew **5.5×**, from 44,149 to 244,510, over those same complete months.

It's a strong chart. I also want the chart to say exactly what it is.

## The question that made the growth worth watching

A search program for a niche product can collect lots of visits from people who will never buy. That's why I care more about the *kind* of question than the graph shape.

Commercial insurance buyers look for workflows, comparisons and alternatives before they reach a product page. A page that helps them understand a real quoting job has a different role from a generic "what is insurance" definition. The former can lead to a conversation about the product. The latter may never get close.

That is the working thesis behind QuoteSweep's search work: meet people in the decision they are already trying to make, then give them a clear next step. The click graph is evidence of growing Google discovery. It does not, by itself, prove which page or tactic caused the lift.

## What the numbers do and don't show

The five bars above are the complete-month counts currently published in the [ankur.works search case file](/seo-ai-search/#evidence): 96, 130, 368, 716 and 988 clicks. August had roughly 5.5 times April's impressions. The site also reports that QuoteSweep pages were observed in repeated Google AI Overview checks for commercial-rater comparisons, EZLynx alternatives and Bold Penguin alternatives.

Those are separate measures. The Search Console chart counts Google clicks. The answer captures record appearances in a defined sample. Neither is a count of sales, leads or revenue. Neither proves that an AI answer caused a specific click.

## The part I would investigate next

I would split the growth into query and page classes.

Which pages earned the additional clicks? How much came from the brand name, and how much from non-brand buyer questions? Did comparison pages grow, or did one glossary term account for most of the chart? Did people move from those pages to the product?

These are answerable questions with a page/query export and on-site events. The aggregate chart cannot answer them. I would rather leave that section open than write a neat story about a particular tactic without the change log.

The same goes for timing. A rise after a page change is a clue. To credit the change, I need the publication dates, URL history, query shifts and competing explanations. Search does not hand out clean causal experiments very often.

## What a useful search case file looks like

For a product like QuoteSweep, I would keep four things on one page:

1. A complete-month discovery chart, with the exact Search Console filters.
2. The pages and query classes that moved, including branded and non-branded splits.
3. A dated record of what changed on the site.
4. The next action: inquiry, beta request or product use, with its own source.

That is the case file I want to keep improving. The 10.3× result earns attention. The page and query detail will tell us what to build next.

If your product is getting impressions but you cannot tell which buyer questions matter, [send me the site and one question you care about](/seo-ai-search/#inquiry). That's where I'd start.
