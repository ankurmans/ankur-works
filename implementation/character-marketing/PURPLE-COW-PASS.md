# Purple Cow pass — mascot branding page

**Scope:** editorial and visual review of `/mascot-branding/`, 3 October 2026. This applies the [Purple Cow Marketing skill](https://github.com/guia-matthieu/clawfu-skills/blob/main/skills/content/purple-cow-marketing/SKILL.md) as a judgment framework, not as a claim of measured word of mouth.

## The repeatable sentence

Ankur builds characters for his own software, then puts them to work in product education and short films.

## The edge and the audience

The page's advantage is **characters doing real jobs in owned products**. Quincy guides Whooshly users; Pepys explains transcription features; both have finished films visitors can play. The first useful audience is software founders and product marketers who need to explain an unfamiliar product repeatedly. The artwork can interest designers and creators, but the buyer needs to see what the character *does*.

The safe middle in this category is a cute mascot gallery followed by generic promises of memorable marketing. This page can show the product context, film, and repeatable creative format instead. That is the story someone could retell. It is not evidence that the mascots raised signups, recognition, or revenue.

## What changed

| Test | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Five-second explanation | The hero's proof note named the two products without the reason to keep looking. | It says “Two products. Two characters. Both in motion.” The case studies supply the proof. |
| Visible difference | Four colored process cards had large blank centers and could belong to almost any creative studio. | Each card now shows an artifact from the actual Pepys and Whooshly work: characters, a role/voice/form sheet, two film frames, and recurring feature scenes. The colors stay. |
| Worth sharing | The strongest shareable material was already the click-to-play Quincy and Pepys films. | The process cards now point back to that work instead of competing with it as decoration. |

## Remarkability check

- **Easy to explain:** yes. One person can say, “He puts characters into the products he builds, then gives them films and repeatable roles.”
- **Visible:** yes. Full-body characters, product illustrations, and both finished films are on the page.
- **Worth the social risk of recommending:** provisionally. The work is viewable and tied to products, but there is no independent client result or audience study.
- **Memorable:** the moving pair and the “character with a job” idea have more recall potential than the generic four-stage labels.
- **Shareable:** partially. Visitors can share the page, but each film lacks a dedicated visible share link. Add direct film links if people begin sharing the examples.

## What to watch

The clearest commercial test is whether visitors who play a film continue to the inquiry, not whether they merely scroll past a colorful card. Track film plays by asset and inquiry source. No conversion or word-of-mouth outcome is asserted here. Do not add invented reach figures, client logos, or mascot-attributed growth to make the page sound louder.
