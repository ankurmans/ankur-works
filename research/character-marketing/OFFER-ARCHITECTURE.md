# Offer architecture

Buyer: founder-led software, AI products and apps that need a memorable way to explain or repeatedly market a product. A product with an existing but underused character is also a fit. Schools, sports teams and costume buyers are outside scope.

The commercial promise is an owned creative system that can be used repeatedly, **not** guaranteed reach, conversion lift, legal clearance or viral distribution.

| Stage | Buyer situation | Working deliverables | Decision gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Character sprint | No character or unclear role | Product/audience brief, visual territory, character concept, personality, expressions, first use ideas | Approve a character that fits the product and can carry multiple scenes |
| Launch kit | Character approved; launch or announcement due | Hero visual, short film or motion piece, product/landing visuals and channel crops | Approve story and product truth before production |
| Content engine | Character and format proved useful | Recurring product stories, shorts, social/campaign assets, seasonal or reactive briefs | Agree cadence, channels and outcome measures for each period |

Operating system: **create → codify → activate → compound**. The first two stages prevent a one-off cute drawing. The latter two make the character useful across launch, product education and distribution.

Measurement plan: page source, offer CTA, video play, inquiry, qualified lead, proposal, closed project. For a client engagement, choose the goal before publishing and measure channel engagement, branded search or product visits with appropriate baselines. Do not attribute overall product growth to a mascot without a credible design.

No public pricing is proposed yet. Scope, number of concepts, animation complexity and usage rights need a real brief. Contract and legal review must define ownership and any trademark/IP clearance; neither is promised on the page.
